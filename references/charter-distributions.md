# Charter distributions: stimulus, compensation and launch-token drops

A charter-related distribution is a separate claim or allocation with its own asset, historical beneficiary and conditions—not ordinary recurring issuance, a universal holder entitlement, reserve redemption or automatic insurance. The October 19, 2026 review distinguishes publisher explanations and dated contract bindings from proven individual entitlement, funding provenance and payment. Use the existing [identity guidance](contracts.md) and [read-only safety boundary](safety.md#preparation-and-wallet-boundary); a public claim inspection needs no wallet connection.

Evidence: [charter-distribution source records](../assets/sources/charter-distributions.json) cover the October 11 stimulus, October 15 burn compensation and publisher claim bindings; [launchpad sources](../assets/sources/launchpad.json) and the [official launchpad guide](https://www.standardreserve.xyz/docs/launchpad/) cover generation-specific third-party drops. Published mechanics do not certify every deployed implementation or beneficiary entry. Live balances, claim deadlines and loading/sealed states are not installed as defaults here.

## Stimulus and top-up

The [October 11 official announcement](https://x.com/standard_rsv/status/2109227448399433909) described a one-time distribution of accumulated **STANDARD surplus earned through protocol-owned-liquidity fees**. Published eligibility required a charter to have purchased **at least one branch through open-market auctions**. The approximately **0.7× issuance-equivalent** description applied to that distribution, not a new ongoing issuance multiplier or guaranteed future accrual rate. The same announcement's issuance heading toward the 0.2× floor is a different claim; the floor, an observed multiplier and this one-off distribution must remain distinct.

Publisher roles `branch_claim` and `branch_top_up`, generation `stimulus-2026-10-11`, distinguish the original stimulus from the top-up. Application copy says the top-up pays what the stimulus's earlier **per-owner cap left out**. The [October 11 developer follow-up](https://x.com/0xbeans/status/2109286629462655439) asked holders with more than ten branches to check again; it does not establish the exact adjustment formula. Do not reconstruct a top-up from today's branch count, assume an uncapped rate, or invent rounding/owner-grouping rules.

The publication attributes funding to prior protocol earnings. An individual amount still needs its claim entry and relevant distribution state; the announcement is neither a beneficiary list nor transfer provenance. It does not make stimulus an ordinary Bank accrual or impose launch-token drop closure rules on these separate claim contracts.

## Burn compensation

The [October 15 developer article, “The Great Burn”](https://x.com/0xbeans/article/2110650614871146854), announced a **one-time, team-earned-fee-funded** claim for tokens lost during dormancy revocation, saying protocol funds would remain untouched. This is distinct from the October 11 protocol-earned POL stimulus. The [official claims-live announcement](https://x.com/standard_rsv/status/2110701828543590735) establishes a published claim route, not execution or individual eligibility.

Keep the two descriptions scoped: reviewed website copy describes a refund of **70% of pending STANDARD lost to the revocation fee**, to wallets that held the charter **at the time it burned**; the developer describes affected holders as being placed in the position of a **full exit at 0% fee**. Do not invent exact gross/net/bounty ordering or assert these descriptions independently prove the same calculated amount. The claim record, authenticated asset scale and relevant historical loss/beneficiary evidence must support a specific answer. Team-fee funding remains an attributed claim unless actual provenance is reconstructed.

Role `charter_refund_claim`, generation `burn-refund-2026-10-15`, is not a restoration mechanism for burned charters. The developer explicitly retained dormancy and rejected a precedent for future unfavorable outcomes: compensation does not remove the 30-day activity rule or create standing insurance. Later October 15 [persistent check-in and warning UI evidence](https://x.com/0xbeans/status/2110703147824136526) concerns visibility, not changed entitlement or a connected-wallet flow exercised in this review. See [charters](charters.md) and [exits](exits.md) for the continuing rules.

## Launchpad charter drops

Launch-token drops are **third-party token allocations**, not STANDARD issuance, protocol-surplus stimulus or team compensation. Authenticate the token's factory/curve relationship and the corresponding `charter_drop` binding through [launchpad generation guidance](launchpad.md#identify-the-token-generation); tokens shown in the same application do not inherit one another's rules.

| Published generation | Reservation and payout scope |
| --- | --- |
| Earlier `launch-2026-10-14` | Up to **10% of published token supply** reserved for charters; its own **per-branch** rules remain in force. Do not assign it the newer exit mode or 80% cap. |
| New `urc5-2026-10-19` | Up to **80% of published token supply** reserved; creator chooses **per-closed-branch or complete-exit** payout at launch. The reviewed guide describes the snapshot and release conditions below. |

For the newer generation, the published guide says branch counts are **frozen at graduation**. If the graduating buy lacks gas to finish the snapshot, anyone can finish it afterward. Graduation alone therefore does not establish a completed snapshot or a collectable allocation. Today's branch count is not the frozen entitlement.

Under the published **per-branch** mode, a charter collects one share for each branch closed below its frozen count, as those branches close. Under **at-exit** mode, its entire allocation becomes collectable only after every branch has closed and the bank has been exited. Authenticate snapshot identity/completion, frozen and closed counts, mode, beneficiary and asset before interpreting a state result; do not substitute current token holdings or a current charter owner for the required historical facts. Do not silently apply this newer snapshot algorithm to the earlier generation without its own evidence.

The newer guide publishes a **one-year return of unclaimed allocations to the protocol**. Establish the actual drop's timing anchor and deadline where exposed rather than counting from the retrieval date or assuming an unlimited claim. These are publisher mechanics, not a whole-implementation certificate. Reserved, allocated, unlocked/collectable and actually received tokens are distinct: an allocation is not immediately spendable merely because shown. Closing a branch or exiting can have separate STANDARD consequences; do not represent a third-party allocation as a free, riskless withdrawal or transfer the drop's mechanics to stimulus/refund claims.

## Inspect an entitlement

Use `charter-distribution-state` for selected stimulus/top-up/refund and earlier-drop reads, `launchpad-drop-snapshot-state` for available newer-drop aggregate fields, and `launchpad-drop-entitlement` for its **unknown per-charter claim scope** in the [capability planning map](capabilities.md#planning-map). The [distribution inventory](interface-inventory-charter-distributions.md) supplies exact layouts, not a mandatory sequence or complete implementation certificate. Snapshot availability does not make entitlement available. If a claim interface or historical entry is missing, block that amount only and continue supported mechanics or evidence observations.

A claim interpretation needs the authenticated contract and generation, asset and decimal scale, historical beneficiary/entry, sealed/loading status, deadline and already-claimed status **where exposed**. Owner and pending owner are different authorities: the October 19 research checkpoint reported a pending refund-contract handoff, not completed acceptance. Refresh relevant state for a current control question; no historical owner, deadline or sealed flag is a current fallback.

Current charter ownership, a visible claim button, or an empty API list does not prove eligibility or ineligibility. Missing entries/ABI coverage, malformed results, reverts and unavailable reads are **not zero entitlement**. Preview query modes, fixture amounts and sample charter IDs in the frontend are demonstration data, never wallet evidence. An incomplete beneficiary census also cannot support “all holders paid.”

Still return supported facts: identify the mechanism, quote scoped published terms and report successfully read entries with their block/time and coverage. A missing deadline or beneficiary amount blocks that dependent eligibility/amount conclusion, not every useful explanation. Keep raw integers and exact token units; do not treat an unknown token price as zero USD value. A successful canonical transaction/receipt and attributable token movements are needed to establish payment, separately from a claim flag or UI success message. Explain consequences read-only; do not connect, sign, approve, prepare an action without an explicit preparation request, or submit a claim.
