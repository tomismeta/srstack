## Two different auctions

| Dimension | Charter auction | Branch-license auction |
|---|---|---|
| Acquired object | A new charter | Additional branches inside an existing charter |
| Evidenced purchase denomination | ETH; event price is raw wei | STANDARD-denominated ledger consideration; authenticate scale and funding semantics |
| Purchase event | `CharterPurchased(charterId, buyer, day, price)` | `LicensesPurchased(charterId, day, count, unitPrice)` |
| Quantities | Charter purchases, identified by charter ID | Purchased branches (`count`), distinct from purchase-event count |
| Availability and constraints | This charter-auction deployment's supply, reserve price, schedule and permissions | This license-auction deployment's inventory, quote, charter capacity, allowance windows and order semantics |

Label tables, prices and scenarios by auction family and generation. Similar method names (`currentPrice`, `remainingToday`, `lastSalePrice`) do not make their units or objects interchangeable. Independently establish each family's clock, floor, price-opening rule and supply. An announced matching cadence is not proof that both historical schedules or activation anchors match. Preserve exact raw prices and scale-derived decimal strings; optional display rounding must not alter the executed amount. Keep ETH and STANDARD totals separate.

“Buy a charter” does not mean “buy branches for my charter.” Infer the intended family from context; ask only when a remaining ambiguity changes the answer. Earned ledger credit is not ETH available for a charter purchase. Any conversion/withdrawal scenario needs its own eligibility, costs and earning-capacity effects, not a 1:1 substitution.

## Four price shapes and history-first forecasts

Disambiguate every auction price answer using these four shapes; do not silently substitute one for another:

When “auction price” has no established family or price meaning in context, ask a concise clarification or show all four labelled shapes, leaving unsupported values unknown. Do not default to a current-curve lookup. Even a correct sold-out/current-quote answer does not silently stand in for the opening, last sale or floor.

| Shape | Meaning and limit |
| --- | --- |
| **Policy opening** | A conditional start-price scenario from the relevant family's policy and qualified prior close. It is not an expected transaction price. The applicable future floor/reset settings may be unknown. |
| **Current inventory-conditional quote** | A same-block quote for the authenticated effective round, conditional on inventory and independent purchase gates. If `remainingToday() == 0`, any residual curve value is diagnostic only, not a buyable price. |
| **Observed last sold** | An executed print tied to its contract, generation, round and block. It is provisional for an open/incompletely covered round; a qualified exhausted close requires historically effective capacity, sale-driven exhaustion and complete ordered coverage. |
| **Current floor; future floor unknown** | A current or historical floor observation has a time and effective round. Historical floor trends provide context, not a known next floor; do not carry today's floor forward as fact. |

**History first is normative for market-price forecasts:** consult the latest relevant curated dataset before deriving a policy opening. Verify its family, units, contract/generation identities, pinned head and coverage, and account for intervening rounds or setting changes. Neither a recently modified file nor an old real dataset is automatically a fresh forecasting cohort. The [round-dataset guide](round-datasets.md) describes durable research storage outside the installed skill and explicit missing values. The installed synthetic example demonstrates arithmetic only; it is never a live default.

Policy rules describe the mechanism that sets an opening; observed history describes market outcomes. For “what will it cost?” questions, use the historical outcome trend to form the market-price estimate and the policy rule as a structural cross-check, never as a substitute answer. Compare (1) a trailing chronological last-sold trend and (2) an explicit policy opening multiplied by the observed sellout close/open ratio distribution, with its ±1 sample-standard-deviation band. Report sample identities/count, chronology, qualification/exclusions, exact units and assumptions, particularly continuation of sellout and comparable inventory/settings. Treat last-observed trends as such; do not promote a partially covered print to a proved close. Divergence between the two projections is information, not a reason to force agreement.

The optional [calculation recipes](calculations.md) expose `close_trend_projection`, `sellout_ratio_summary`, `structural_close_projection`, `floor_trend_context` and `project_next_close`. Choose and disclose the trailing sample and policy multiplier; there is no fixed live ratio or per-round decrement. Sample standard deviation describes empirical dispersion, not a confidence interval or contractual regularity. Keep numerical error bounds separate. Future floor remains unknown, and a negative extrapolation must be reported as model failure rather than clipped into a plausible price. If history is unavailable, partial, stale or insufficiently qualified, explain the precise limit rather than inventing a numeric market-close forecast or confusing the policy opening with one.

For alternative forecast assumptions, [collect once and calculate locally](collection-efficiency.md#where-this-applies): reuse the qualified history and anchor rather than rerunning retrieval per scenario. A changed evidence scope or requested refresh remains a separately identified collection.

If demand breaks, a round may not sell out and its last print can occur anywhere down to its applicable floor; a no-sale round has no last-sold price. The future floor itself remains unknown. “Every capped round sold out” must identify the covered historical sample, not become a perpetual demand assumption.

## Current license availability and next opportunity

For “can I buy now?”, establish the relevant availability gate before interpreting a price. Use authenticated generation-specific state at one pinned block and [discover the current deployment/interface](contracts.md); query only what the question needs. An authenticated effective `remainingToday() == 0` means **not buyable now**, regardless of a positive `currentPrice()` or `quote(...)`: that output can be a leftover curve value, not an executable ask. Unavailable or sold-out is not zero-cost; missing state stays unknown, never an announcement default.

Use the [shared collection recipe](collection-efficiency.md) for independent same-pin availability, allowance, capacity and configuration reads. Resolve target/clock dependencies first and collect only what the question requires; a supported batch is not a reason to continue an unrelated full snapshot after a binding constraint is known.

Distinguish open protocol branches (`totalBranches()`), per-round license allocation/cap, effective remaining inventory, per-charter purchase allowance and open orders. The [dated interface guide](interface-guide.md) supplies generation-qualified leads, not active targets. `openBidCount()` counts orders, not licenses; a bid may request multiple units. A historical allocation does not override live state. Authenticate denomination before displaying prices.

Stored `currentDay`/`soldToday` can describe the last materialized round while a view reports effective availability for an elapsed round. Reconcile `auctionAnchor`, `auctionPeriod`, block time, stored round and authenticated lazy-roll/getter semantics before interpreting zero inventory. Never assume `remaining = cap - stored sold` across mismatched rounds or use a stale round counter as effective `N`. Method names do not establish calendar/reset semantics; apparent mismatch is not proof of unreliable getters. Check same-block charter allowance such as `remainingForCharter`, remaining capacity `MAX_BRANCHES − branchCount`, and relevant pause/started gates when needed to assess a positive opportunity. One independently proved binding zero is enough to answer “not now”; do not fetch every other field merely to repeat that conclusion.

Zero effective availability establishes the binding **unavailable-now** result, not its cause: zero allocation, not-started state, a pause or sale-driven exhaustion need separate evidence. Do not call a round sold out or infer a closing sale merely from a zero getter; a no-purchase round has no closing sale price.

### Qualified round lifecycle

For authenticated effective round `N`, the scheduled window is `[auctionAnchor + N × auctionPeriod, auctionAnchor + (N + 1) × auctionPeriod)`. Buying ends when authenticated effective `remainingToday() == 0`; price decay does not reopen inventory. In the observed lifecycle below, the getter is time-based with fixed round settings and continues decaying independently of inventory: its post-exhaustion residual is phantom/not-buyable, not a current offer. Sellout does not advance the day index early; the next scheduled round boundary is still the anchor boundary. Keep that effective schedule distinct from stored/materialized counters and a later roll transaction or first purchase. A zero-allocation/no-sale round has no closing sale; zero inventory alone does not prove sale-driven exhaustion. The next boundary remains conditional on applicable settings and is not a promise of allocation or fill.

**Scoped observation, 2026-09-27:** the user reported verifying license day 0→1 openings at **2×** the previous close and charter day 0→1 openings at **3×**, curve decay continuing hours after zero inventory, and `currentDay` remaining unchanged until the scheduled boundary. These reports support the corresponding conditional publisher-policy scenarios and the no-early-roll lifecycle in the observed scope. They are not independent verification here, a universal all-generation implementation certificate, proof of every historical close, or evidence of future settings. Preserve any corroborating contract/block/raw-value evidence with its scope; do not infer unreported rounds or invent a historical dataset.

If `N` is the authenticated effective zero-based current round and the effective clock remains applicable, the next scheduled window is `[anchor + (N + 1) × period, anchor + (N + 2) × period)`. It is a **next candidate opportunity**, not guaranteed replenishment, eligibility, allowance reset, price or fill. A charter's allowance clock may differ; its remaining capacity does not reset with the auction. If the clock or effective round is unresolved, report that specific timing gap instead of adding a period to a delayed roll transaction.

For past purchases or round tables, use [history](auction-history.md#question-to-window): discover every relevant generation, intersect requested and deployment bounds, estimate provider workload before collection, and use a suitable independently authorized index/wide-range log path with finite retries and coverage checkpoints. Do not default to genesis or launch thousands of tiny chunks from a range-limit response. Reused round IDs never merge across contracts. A current round's purchases are through-observation totals, not a completed recap; logs do not replace current availability or open-order reads. Transaction input minus event price is not proof of a refund; verify and label payment, refund and gas evidence separately.

For affordability, establish the charter's remaining allowance and capacity separately from inventory and the budget. A new auction round need not reset its fixed-window allowance. A next-24-hours forecast can cross a reset after prior usage; apply limits at each effective boundary rather than treating the current remaining allowance or a published per-window cap as a universal rolling-day limit. An earned-only request additionally needs attributable unspent earnings and horizon accrual, not total pending with a disclaimer. Future inventory, prices and execution remain assumptions unless established. A funds-only bound is useful but is not an executable purchase count.

Lead a sold-out answer with **not now and the binding reason**, then the next evidenced candidate window, then the qualified budget comparison. Keep three comparators separate: a **dated license-policy opening scenario**, **recent receipt-verified sale prices**, and a **conditional unsold-curve/budget intersection**. The opening rule is not today's ask, recent prints are not available offers, and the intersection assumes inventory and other gates remain open. Apply [empirical pace and dilution scenarios](research-workflow.md#empirical-pending-delta-pace) only with their observation/assumption labels and epoch/issuance clipping; do not offer a single unsourced purchase date. Avoid unsolicited limit-order or keeper advice and unrelated calls; this is not a fixed query-count playbook.

## Next license opening: documented-policy estimate

The [whitepaper §7](https://www.standardreserve.xyz/app/protocol/whitepaper/#branches), freshly reviewed **2026-09-27**, states “2× the previous auction's closing sale price” or, after no sales, “twice the new floor”; [§8](https://www.standardreserve.xyz/app/protocol/whitepaper/#auctions) adds “subject to the current floor.” These are dated publisher rules, not authenticated implementation or current execution configuration.

For a market forecast, apply the [history-first principle](#four-price-shapes-and-history-first-forecasts) before calculating this opening. If asked for a policy scenario, label it and expose its inputs. A latest sale from an open round is provisional; retained last-sale state may belong to an older round. A receipt-verified last sale can serve as the round's exhausted closing sale reference only when historical inventory exhaustion and complete ordered purchase coverage are authenticated. Sold-out storage alone does not supply that proof. A future floor is not today's stored floor. Use the **license** rule—2× the evidenced previous close, subject to the applicable floor—as a dated policy scenario, never the charter 3× rule or an implementation-verified next quote. Exact current-opening claims require authenticated reset/floor semantics and compatible inputs; otherwise preserve the conditional model and withhold only the unsupported factual claim. The scoped transition observation above does not remove those limits.

## Next license timing: schedule versus transaction

After establishing zero-based round-index semantics, use the scheduled window `[auctionAnchor + N × auctionPeriod, auctionAnchor + (N + 1) × auctionPeriod)`. Read the applicable generation's timing interface; current v1.1/v1.2 leads include `auctionPeriod()`, not a blanket historical “reverts” rule. No hardcoded 12-hour fallback. Historical configuration changes need their applicable effect established. State unchanged-configuration assumptions and any pause/inactive/pending-roll state. A scheduled boundary is not a keeper transaction or first/last sale time; adding a period to a late roll transaction misdates the schedule.

A repeated-demand scenario can compare a future opportunity with a prior round's elapsed **scheduled-opening-to-exhaustion/last-sale** time under explicit comparable-demand, inventory and schedule assumptions. First-to-last purchase span omits any wait before the first buyer and is not time from opening to sellout. Historical last-sale time is an execution timestamp, not the scheduled closing boundary. Show a bounded conditional case, not promised sellout timing; use [historical license comparators](auction-history.md#historical-license-comparators).

## Published price rule versus deployed implementation

Current [whitepaper §7](https://www.standardreserve.xyz/app/protocol/whitepaper/#branches) explicitly gives `P_floor = 2 × (I_base × m / N)` and `P(t) = P_floor + (P_start - P_floor) × 2^(-t / 2h)`: the license price's **gap to the floor** halves every two hours. `I_base` is the daily base issuance, `m` the policy multiplier and `N` total branches; 700,000 is the launch base, not an immutable current input. `license-floor-yield-days` records the two-day yield interpretation.

This is a usable published modeling rule, superseding the earlier whitepaper's conflicting license-curve descriptions. It is not authenticated deployed code, exact integer arithmetic, floor-update timing or a purchasable quote. Use live getters for current observations and interval-specific implementation/state evidence for exact historical calculations.

Requested source-rule comparisons and time-to-buy models may use these published curves with explicit inventory, demand and future-setting assumptions under [modeling boundaries](risks.md#what-economics-alone-cannot-establish). Buying earlier versus waiting for a lower price is a tradeoff, not guaranteed allocation or returns. Current owner-adjustable settings require live reads; unavailable state remains unknown.
